## Source-stated rules

### Items 5 and 6 — Masking participants, carers, and intervention providers

Participant masking (item 5) and masking carers or intervention deliverers (item 6) will be `No` in almost all psychotherapy trials. Possible exceptions require close examination, such as two active psychological interventions presented as equally effective or a non-inferiority design.

### Item 7 — Important deviations caused by the trial context

When item 5 or 6 is `No/PN` or `NI`, ask whether the interventions still closely followed plan without deviations capable of changing the result.

A relevant deviation has three features: it is associated with the lack of masking or trial context, can influence the outcome, and is meaningfully unbalanced between groups. Examples include wait-list participants obtaining the experimental psychotherapy outside the trial, or one group receiving substantially more influential co-intervention such as antidepressants.

Do not count a deviation merely because it occurred. A deviation that would also arise outside the trial, such as stopping an antidepressant because of side effects, does not by itself bias an intention-to-treat effect. A co-intervention also must plausibly affect the outcome; the handbook contrasts potentially non-influential benzodiazepine use in a depression trial with receiving the target psychotherapy or antidepressant medication.

Balanced influential deviations are less concerning. Review teams should pre-agree how to define imbalance; the source offers a 20 percentage-point imbalance as a rough signal. Where influential deviations are sufficiently unbalanced, answer `No/PN`.

### Item 8 — Analysis appropriate for the intention-to-treat effect

The target is analysis according to original randomized assignment. The HTML treats reported intention-to-treat (ITT), and modified ITT that excludes participants only because outcomes are missing, as appropriate for this item. It is inappropriate to exclude participants because they did not receive or sufficiently adhere to their assigned intervention, or to analyze them under the intervention actually received. An explicitly stated ITT label is not always necessary when the reported method inherently follows ITT principles.

### Item 9 — Likely impact of an inappropriate analysis

If item 8 is `No/PN` or `NI`, evaluate how many randomized participants were excluded for adherence/receipt reasons or analyzed in the wrong arm:

- below 5%: answer `Yes/PY`, yielding some concerns under the algorithm;
- above 5%: answer `No/PN`;
- insufficient information: answer `NI`.

Ordinary missing endpoint questionnaires are assessed in [domain 3](domain-3-missing-data.md), not here.

## Working synthesis

Separate deviations induced by awareness of assignment from ordinary real-world nonadherence. Then ask whether those deviations could affect the target outcome and were unbalanced. Independently, check whether the analysis preserved randomized assignment; use item 9 only to judge the likely effect of a failure on item 8.
