The page is also labeled “Selection of the reported result” on some source surfaces. Its six items are the same. Evaluation must follow the meta-analysis protocol: a project seeking every measure of a domain has different completeness requirements from one using a pre-specified instrument hierarchy.

## Source-stated rules

### Item 19 — Availability of registration, protocol, or statistical analysis plan

If reviewers cannot access a document defining outcomes or analyses in advance, answer `NI`, stop the domain sequence, and assign some concerns. If a document is available, continue.

### Item 20 — Record the source document

Record the trial registration number, protocol, or statistical analysis plan (SAP) link so the judgment is auditable.

### Item 21 — Prospective specification of outcome scale and time point

Determine whether relevant outcomes were specified before unmasked outcome data became available. The handbook operationalizes this through prospective registration, comparing recruitment start with registration posting; it describes outcome specification before data collection and uses a one-month window around the start of enrollment. Some registries, including ISRCTN, directly indicate prospective status.

Inspect registry change history for post hoc modifications. Ideally the record defines outcome domain, scale, time point, metric, and analysis, but scale and time point are the minimum stated basis for `Yes`. Retrospective registration, or a prospective record that omits scale/time, leads to `NI` and some concerns.

### Item 22 — Complete availability of outcomes required by the review protocol

Judge completeness against the meta-analysis protocol. If all instruments in a domain are eligible, every eligible instrument needs usable result data. If the protocol defines a hierarchy (for example, one depression scale ahead of another), judge the result selected under that hierarchy.

Adding an unregistered outcome in the publication is `No/PN` and high risk. Data obtained through another route, including author contact, can count as available.

### Item 23 — Prospective analysis plan

An analysis plan must predate unblinded outcome information. The source says publication of the plan should precede enrollment; for a published protocol, its acceptance date should precede enrollment. Use `NI` when it is unclear whether the document reflects a genuinely pre-specified plan.

### Item 24 — Adherence to the pre-specified analysis

Deviations should be disclosed and justified in the final report. A well-justified deviation may remain low risk after considering its effect on the target result. The prose says item 24 normally does not affect the domain algorithm unless there is evidence of an unjustified deviation; in that case reviewers should consider high risk. The [published decision rows](algorithms.md#domain-5) explicitly make item 24=`No` override item 22=`Yes` or `NI` to high risk.

## Working synthesis

Anchor eligible outcomes and analyses in the review protocol, locate the earliest timestamped trial record, inspect its history, and compare the final result with both the outcome specification and analysis plan. Missing prospective documentation produces some concerns; evidence of outcome suppression, an unregistered added outcome, or unjustified analytic deviation can produce high risk.
