## Source-stated rules

### Item 15 — Appropriate and comparable measurement

The instrument must validly and reliably measure the meta-analysis outcome domain, and both groups must be measured using similar procedures.

### Item 16 — Self-report measures

This item is tagged for `createRobRatings`. The handbook does not automatically penalize self-report simply because psychotherapy participants usually cannot be blinded. In the absence of evidence of under-reporting—and especially where safeguards keep therapists from seeing responses—self-report may be judged low risk.

Answer `No/PN` when under-reporting is plausible, such as participants completing the final assessment in front of a therapist and minimizing symptoms to please the therapist or researchers. Under the stated algorithm this can make domain 4 high risk.

### Item 17 — Assessor-rated measures

This item is tagged for `createRobRatings`. Assessor-rated instruments include structured interviews or behavioral observations completed by therapists, independent evaluators, or research staff.

### Item 18 — Masking outcome assessors

When item 17 is `Yes`, answer item 18 `Yes` only when the report clearly states that assessors were masked to allocation. If masking is not reported, the handbook says it can be reasonable to assume `No/PN`.

## Multiple measurements of one outcome domain

When a trial supplies both self-report and assessor-rated results for the same outcome domain, the source permits two protocol-dependent strategies:

- rate each measurement and numeric result separately; or
- combine measurement-level ratings and conservatively make the aggregate high risk if any component is high risk.

The MetaPsy depression database uses the second strategy. The choice should follow the meta-analysis protocol rather than being made after seeing the risk judgments.

## Working synthesis

Start with validity and equal procedures. Then classify the actual result as self-report, assessor-rated, or a protocol-defined aggregate. Self-report is not inherently high risk; likely response distortion is the key concern. For assessor ratings, documented masking is the key discriminator.
